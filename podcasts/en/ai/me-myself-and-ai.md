# Me, Myself, and AI

## 简介

**Me, Myself, and AI** is a podcast on artificial intelligence adoption in business, jointly produced by **MIT Sloan Management Review (MIT SMR)** and **Boston Consulting Group (BCG)**. The show is hosted by **Sam Ransbotham** (professor of analytics at Boston College and MIT SMR guest editor) and **Shervin Khodabandeh** (BCG managing director and partner, leader of BCG's AI practice in North America). It is a long-form, business-focused AI podcast that focuses on the "human + AI" dimension of enterprise AI adoption, rather than on the technical details of the models.

The show launched in 2021 amid the post-ChatGPT enterprise AI wave and has since become a flagship property of MIT SMR's growing audio portfolio. Unlike AI podcasts that focus on models, papers, or AI labs, *Me, Myself, and AI* is structured around the question: "How are real companies using AI today, what is working, and what is failing?" The audience is enterprise leaders, AI strategists, and consultants — not engineers.

The show's name is a play on the doubling / collaboration theme: it's not "Me vs. AI" or "AI vs. Humans" but "Me + AI" — emphasizing that AI adoption is fundamentally about how humans and machines work together, and that the interesting questions are about the collaboration, not the replacement.

## Hosts Background

- **Sam Ransbotham**:professor of analytics at the Carroll School of Management at Boston College, and a long-time guest editor / contributor at *MIT Sloan Management Review*. Sam is known for his work on data science / analytics education, AI ethics, and the organizational dimensions of AI adoption. He co-authored the well-known "Reshaping Business with Artificial Intelligence" MIT SMR / BCG research series, which surveyed thousands of executives on AI maturity.
- **Shervin Khodabandeh**:managing director and partner at **BCG**, leader of BCG's AI practice in North America, and a co-author of the MIT SMR / BCG AI research series. Shervin has spent 20+ years working with Fortune 500 companies on AI strategy, with deep experience in supply chain, pricing, and operations analytics. He is known for translating AI capabilities into business outcomes in a way that resonates with C-suite audiences.

The pairing of an academic (Sam) and a practitioner (Shervin) gives the show a unique lens: Sam brings the research-grounded questions, Shervin brings the "what actually works in the enterprise" perspective.

## Content Style and Format

- **Format**:single 30–45 minute interview per episode, with occasional "roundtable" or "panel" episodes.
- **Frequency**:biweekly, with a steady cadence.
- **Structure**:each episode follows a similar arc — guest's background, the company's AI journey (from pilot to scale), the human + collaboration angle, the failures / lessons learned, and the future of the work. This consistent structure makes the show easy to follow across episodes.
- **Tone**:thoughtful, business-focused, non-hype. The hosts are not trying to "sell" AI to the audience; they are trying to help the audience understand what works and what doesn't. This makes the show a useful counterweight to more hype-driven AI podcasts.
- **Distribution**:the show is hosted on the MIT SMR website, with audio mirrored to Apple Podcasts, Spotify, YouTube, and the major podcast platforms via RSS.
- **Companion research**:the show is a companion to the MIT SMR / BCG annual AI research series ("Reshaping Business with AI"), which is a major reference for enterprise AI maturity.

## Audience Profile

- **Enterprise AI strategists and consultants** — the show's primary audience, who use the show to track how other companies are approaching AI adoption.
- **C-suite and senior managers** — the show's emphasis on "what works in the enterprise" makes it accessible and useful for non-technical senior leaders.
- **AI product managers and AI transformation leads** — particularly those working in large enterprises that are mid-pilot or mid-scale.
- **Business school students and faculty** — the show is widely used in MBA and executive education programs on AI strategy.
- **General business readers of MIT SMR** — the show is a natural extension of the publication's editorial scope.

## Influence and Reviews

- **Top-tier business AI podcast**:the show is one of the most-cited English-language business AI podcasts, and a flagship property of MIT SMR's growing audio portfolio.
- **Research-backed**:the show's companion research (the MIT SMR / BCG AI maturity series) is a major reference for enterprise AI maturity, and the show regularly references and expands on this research.
- **Cross-media impact**:the show's interviews are frequently cited in MIT SMR articles, BCG reports, and academic case studies on AI adoption.
- **Cultural footprint**:the show has helped popularize the "human + AI collaboration" framing in the enterprise AI conversation, alongside books like *Human + Machine* (Daugherty & Wilson, 2018) and *Power and Prediction* (Agrawal, Gans, & Goldfarb, 2022).
