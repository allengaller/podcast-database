# Latent Space

## Summary

**"The AI Engineer Podcast"** — by and for AI engineers. News, papers, and interviews focused on the models, tools, and ideas that practitioners use at work the next day. One of the most influential AI-engineering-native shows, paired with a widely-read newsletter and Discord community.

The show coined the framing of the **"AI Engineer"** as a distinct role (vs. ML engineer / data scientist), and its annual State of AI Engineering survey/report has become a reference document for the ecosystem. The "Latent Space" name comes from the ML term for the abstract representation space in which models operate.

## Hosts

- **swyx (Shawn Wang)** — AI engineer turned writer/community builder; previously at AWS (serverless), now full-time on Latent Space; also writes the popular *Pragmatic Engineer* newsletter's AI section and is a frequent speaker at AI Engineer Summit / World's Fair.
- **Alessio Fanelli** — Investor at Decibel Partners (a seed-stage fund focused on dev tools, infrastructure, and AI), brings the VC + technical lens; previously at Apple (Maps) and multiple startups.

> Both hosts identify the audience as "builders" — engineers shipping AI products, not researchers or generalists.

## Format

- **Category**: AI / AI engineering
- **Format**: Host + guest interviews (researchers, founders, infra builders, open-source maintainers)
- **Cadence**: Weekly+
- **Episode length**: ~60–90 minutes (long form)
- **Language**: English
- **Distribution**: Audio (Apple/Spotify) + Video (YouTube) + full **written transcript + show notes on latent.space** (the website is itself a reference archive)
- **Adjacent assets**: AI Engineer Summit conference (in-person), AI Engineer World's Fair, annual *State of AI Engineering* report, Discord community

## Audience

AI engineers, ML practitioners, infra / dev-tools founders, and technical investors wanting grounded, practitioner-level signal on the model / tooling ecosystem. Distinct from general "AI podcasts" (e.g. NVIDIA's, Sam Altman's) in that Latent Space is uninterested in hype; its typical question is "what does this mean for what I should ship on Monday?"

## Notable episodes (representative, by theme)

- **Code generation / agents** — Multiple deep dives with Cody (Codeium), Cursor, Devin, and Cognition leadership; Latent Space covered the emergence of "vibe coding" / agentic engineering workflows earlier than most.
- **Computer vision era** — Episode with Joseph Nelson (RoboFlow) on Segment Anything, custom CV workflows, and how SAM changed the build-vs-buy calculation for vision startups.
- **Open source model launches** — Frequent early interviews with leadership of Mistral, Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek, OLMo, and others on the practical trade-offs of self-hosting vs. API.
- **Evals & observability** — The *"What we learned building a production LLM app"* series (Braintrust, Honeycomb, LangSmith, Arize) became de facto required listening for AI reliability engineers.
- **AI Engineer Summit talks** — Many conference talks get re-cut into Latent Space episodes (Simon Willison, Hamel Husain, Eugene Yan, Jason Liu, etc.).

## Reach & assessment

- Considered the **reference podcast for the AI-engineering subfield**; cited frequently by practitioners in talks and blog posts.
- The associated newsletter has tens of thousands of subscribers; the *State of AI Engineering* annual report is one of the most widely shared ecosystem surveys.
- Business model: sponsorships + paid community / newsletter + AI Engineer Summit ticketing.
- Reputation for **technical depth without gatekeeping** — the hosts will dig into evals, embeddings, RAG architecture, and inference cost as comfortably as a research podcast, but in builder-shorthand.
- The show's Twitter/X account is one of the most-followed AI-engineering accounts; its "TIL" (today I learned) clip series is widely reposted.
