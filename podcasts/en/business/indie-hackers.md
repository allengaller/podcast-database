# Indie Hackers

## Summary

A long-form interview podcast for "indie hackers" — independent founders building profitable, often bootstrapped, online businesses without taking VC money. Sits at the center of the bootstrapping / lifestyle-business / Micro-SaaS movement that grew out of Hacker News circles in the mid-2010s. Tied to the IndieHackers.com community (now a Stripe property) which aggregates case studies, revenue figures, and tactics from independent builders.

## Hosts

- **Courtland Allen** — Founder of IndieHackers.com (launched **2016-02**); sold the community to **Stripe in 2017**; remains the face of the brand. Earlier background in software / community engineering.
- **Channing Allen** — Courtland's sister, joined as co-host in 2020; helps format episodes and runs the show's writing/community surfaces.
- The site/community and the podcast are distinct but coupled: community members are also frequent guests.

## Format

- **Category**: Bootstrapping / indie founders / SaaS / digital products
- **Format**: Long-form host + founder interview (typically solo, 1:1)
- **Cadence**: Weekly (with intermittent breaks; the show has produced 270+ episodes since 2017)
- **Episode length**: ~50–90 minutes
- **Language**: English
- **Companion artifacts**: every episode publishes a detailed write-up at IndieHackers.com (transcript, key links, follow-up interviews with referenced founders), and the show is paired with the **IndieHackers Newsletter** (weekly, Thursdays).

## Audience

Indie hackers, solo founders, Micro-SaaS builders, designers/devs shipping side-projects, and bootstrappers who want tactics from others who have hit meaningful revenue (often $1k–$100k MRR). Strong overlap with the Hacker News / r/Entrepreneur / "Build in Public" communities.

## Notable episodes / topics

- **Courtland's own journey** — building IndieHackers in public (revenue / traffic disclosures, a transparency pattern later adopted by many indie founders).
- **Pieter Levels (NomadList, RemoteOK)** — the "lifestyle Micro-SaaS" archetype.
- **Tyler Tringas (Storemapper → MicroAcquire)** — bootstrapped Micro-SaaS, then building the acquisition marketplace for indie SaaS.
- **Justin Jackson (Transistor.fm)** — independent podcast hosting; recurring host/infrastructure partner.
- **Andrew Warner (Mixergy)** — pre-existing indie founder-interview canon.
- **Andrew Yeung (Futuresearch / fwd.space)** — community-as-business case studies.
- **KP (Theird.com)** — building in public vs. transparency; community organizing as business model (see "The Threat of AI, Building in Public vs. Transparency" episode).
- Recurring topic threads: pricing Micro-SaaS, hitting $1k → $10k → $100k MRR, finding the first 100 users, indie AI apps, "kill the project" retrospectives.

## Reach & assessment

- **Community / site**: IndieHackers.com — at acquisition in 2017 it was the central hub for the bootstrapping movement; under Stripe ownership it has remained a destination for indie case studies, interviews, and the "IndieHackers Podcast" feed.
- **Podcast**: Among the most-cited podcasts in the bootstrapping canon, frequently named alongside *Startups for the Rest of Us* (Rob Walling), *My First Million* (Sam Parr / Shaan Puri), *Bootstrapped Web* (Brian Casel), and *The SaaS Bootstrapper* (Mac Martine).
- **Hosting / infrastructure**: the show is hosted on **Transistor.fm** — a podcaster-pays-podcaster alignment that signals the indie ethos.
- **Related ecosystem**: the "awesome-indie" GitHub list (~140+ stars, ~1.4k commits as of 2024) names IndieHackers as the catalyst for the modern "make a living from your own code" movement, alongside Hacker News, bootstrapped.fm, and MegaMaker Club.
- **Business model**: site is Stripe-owned; the podcast itself is effectively a brand-marketing channel for the community and the Stripe-built founder ecosystem. No heavy ad-load; sponsorships are minimal and product-aligned (Transistor, MicroAcquire-style).
